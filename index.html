                About Threatline

                Threatline is a free, automated digest of the vulnerabilities attackers are exploiting right now and the security news around them. It sorts everything into red, orange and green, so an analyst can see in a minute what needs attention first.

                Who runs it

                Threatline is built and maintained by Markus Kankare. More of his work is at markuskankare.com and on GitHub. If you spot a mistake or a broken source, please report it there.

                Where the data comes from

                CISA KEV catalogueWhich vulnerabilities are confirmed exploited, the required action, the due date, ransomware use and the weakness type.
                FIRST EPSSThe predicted chance that a vulnerability is exploited in the next 30 days.
                CVE Program recordsCVSS severity, taken from the vendor's score or, if there is none, from CISA's.
                News feedsBleepingComputer, The Record, The Hacker News and CISA advisories. Only the headline, date and link are kept.

                Threatline is not affiliated with or endorsed by any of these organisations. Headlines belong to their publishers.

                How often it updates

                The sources are checked every 2 hours. An open page re-reads the data every 10 minutes, and the time of the data is shown at the top right.

                Limits you should know

                • It ranks general risk. It knows nothing about your systems, so a red item may not affect you and a green one might.
                • News is scored by keywords in the headline, which will sometimes misjudge a story.
                • Very new vulnerabilities often have no CVSS or EPSS score yet. They are scored without it.
                • Threat actor names come from a fixed watchlist, so an actor that is not on it will not appear.
                • The site is provided as is. Confirm against the original source before you act on anything here.

                How the colours are decided

                • Every item gets a score from 0 to 100 from fixed rules. No AI guesses the rank.
                • Vulnerabilities start at 50 for confirmed exploitation, then gain points for EPSS probability, CVSS severity, ransomware use and recency. 70 or more is red, the rest orange.
                • News starts at 20 and gains points for headline signals, links to exploited CVEs and coverage by several sources. 60 or more is red, 40 or more orange, the rest green.
                • Open any item to see which rules gave it its score. The full method is on the About page.
                • The collector checks the sources every 2 hours. This page re-reads the data every 10 minutes, so you can leave it open.

                What this is not

                This ranks general risk. It knows nothing about your systems, so a red item may not affect you and a green one might. Check each against your own assets before acting. Headlines belong to their publishers; follow the links for the full story.
